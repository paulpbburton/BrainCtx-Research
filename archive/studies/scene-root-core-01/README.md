## Current state

The study has three deliberately separate evidence populations:

1. a full-roster commissioning cohort, retained as harness-failure evidence;
2. bounded observation runs, retained as naturalistic trajectory evidence
   under reduced custody; and
3. fork replay, retained as synthetic intervention evidence that never pools
   with the natural runs.

The bounded runner carried work across successor boundaries and made the
program's failure frontier observable. Later review loops repaired defects,
those repairs survived into successor state, and a live-attached detector
could record time-sensitive conditions before the work changed their
referents. The full intended chain nevertheless remains uncompleted.

Exact launch, rung, timing, product, and sensor counts remain in private
custody. The public result is the framework behavior: distinct evidence
classes, explicit force limits, failure preservation, and no conversion of an
incomplete run into a null or success.

## Replay frontier

The replay instrument reconstructed one natural fork under a registration
sealed before any crossing. Controls ran first. An inadmissible arm was
blocked rather than approximated, and a registered observable the instrument
could not collect remained `UNMEASURED` rather than becoming zero.

Two judge-panel rounds then tested the retained single-evaluator record. They
showed that evidence access and binding capability matter, blocked a weak
binding from solo adjudication, and attached a recalibration flag without
rewriting the original rows.
