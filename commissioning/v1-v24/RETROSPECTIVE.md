## Outcome

The cohort did not complete its intended three-rung chain. It therefore did
not observe whether knowledge learned from a predecessor changed a successor's
decision, artifact, or accepted outcome.

It did establish substantial live behavior:

- real planned coding work in isolated repositories;
- independent terminal evaluation;
- bounded same-owner correction with fresh reevaluation;
- a typed continuation that started a cold successor and held it behind a
  knowledge barrier;
- failed-rung learning through two separately recovered knowledge paths;
- and a halt that withheld continuation after product refusal.

The private run roots and detailed evidence remain authoritative and are not
published here.

## Two questions, in order

The cohort asked:

1. **Composition:** can the execution graph complete three dependent rungs
   with honest product inheritance, judgment, learning, custody, and
   continuation?
2. **Knowledge effect:** does predecessor product state and freshly recovered
   knowledge change later work?

The first question must succeed before the second is observable. No attempt
crossed both live successor boundaries.

## How attempts were preserved

Each retry started from declared neutral host and process-knowledge baselines.
A failed root was frozen. Its product, model outputs, and learned state became
evidence, not input to the next attempt.

This protected the learning question from silent cross-attempt contamination.
It also made commissioning expensive: a newly exposed deterministic boundary
could require replaying earlier live work before the next frontier became
visible.

## Failure classes

| Class | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Runner** | Deterministic orchestration, admission, schema, state, namespace, or custody defect. |
| **Model interface** | Containable structured-output, identifier, or namespace mistake at a model boundary. |
| **Provider** | External native-provider transport failure. |
| **Product** | Substantive implementation or test defect found by independent judgment. |
| **Telemetry** | Runtime behavior existed, but strict readout could not type or join it honestly. |

A run may have more than one class. A guard can be correct while the recovery
policy around it is unnecessarily fatal.

## What progressed

### Attempts 1–8: entering the graph

The first attempts established executable custody: provider schemas,
host-derived identifiers, workspace authority, plan claims, and failure
typing. None reached the coding owner.

### Attempts 9–11: keeping knowledge planes separate

Private roles sometimes cited real identifiers from the wrong context or
authority plane. The response was to keep namespaces separate, make the valid
namespace explicit, retain invalid responses, and permit bounded replacement
for containable interface errors.

These attempts provide no coding-result evidence because the coding owner did
not run.

### Attempts 12–16: real coding and continuation

Attempt 12 was the first coding attempt. Attempt 13 completed one dependent item. Attempt 14
completed both planned items and received a substantive verifier acceptance
before local custody checks stopped closure.

Attempt 15 crossed the next frontier.

### Attempt 15: a real continuation frontier

Attempt 15:

- completed both planned items and accepted commands;
- committed the product;
- completed terminal verification and goal/fidelity acceptance;
- ran both knowledge distillers;
- closed rung 1;
- published a continuation decision;
- started a distinct rung-2 process; and
- held that process behind the knowledge barrier without entering its graph.

The barrier failed on a deterministic knowledge-owner path mismatch. Thus attempt 15
demonstrated continuation, a cold successor, and a real wait—not a completed
two-store barrier or successor orientation.

### Attempts 17–24: product fidelity and terminal recovery

Once control-plane seams became less dominant, independent goal/fidelity
review repeatedly found substantive defects after focused tests passed and a
verifier accepted.

### Independent review after green tests

In attempt 24:

1. the initial implementation passed 17 focused tests and the verifier
   accepted;
2. the first correction reached 22 passing tests and a fresh verifier
   accepted;
3. the second correction reached 29 passing tests and another fresh verifier
   accepted; and
4. the final independent review still found an unenforced declared threshold.

This is evidence that independent semantic review improved the attempted
product within this task family. It is not evidence that the final product was
acceptable, that two rounds are generally sufficient, or that the role is
economically justified.

### Attempts 23 and 24: failed-rung learning and safe halt

Both attempts:

- retained the failed product and terminal history;
- ran separately targeted domain and orchestration distillation;
- updated and freshly recovered both knowledge stores;
- published a failed terminal disposition; and
- withheld a successor.

Their failed products remain evidence only.

## Provider and interface observations

Only two attempts ended on external provider overload. Most incomplete runs
occurred after one or more successful native calls and were dominated by
deterministic custody, schema, projection, identifier, namespace, state, or
product boundaries.

Several model-interface failures required exact reproduction of identifiers
or records the host could derive. The durable design lesson was:

```text
ask the model for semantic selection
→ bind selection to a small exact namespace
→ derive canonical authority deterministically
→ return containable errors for bounded replacement
```

Strict evidence and authority checks remain necessary. Redundant formatting
equality is not a substitute for semantic and custody joins.

## What the cohort established

The evidence supports:

1. the composed instrument performs real model, process, workspace, judgment,
   learning, and continuation effects;
2. continuation can start a cold successor and hold it behind a barrier;
3. a failed rung can update and freshly recover two knowledge stores without
   releasing a successor;
4. independent semantic review repeatedly found consequential defects after
   green focused tests and verifier acceptance;
5. bounded same-owner correction can improve an attempted product while
   preserving custody; and
6. runner and interface brittleness, rather than provider outage, caused most
   repeated attempts.

## What the cohort did not establish

The evidence does not support:

1. end-to-end completion of the intended three-rung chain;
2. successful release of an informed successor after both knowledge updates;
3. inherited knowledge changing a successor decision, artifact, or outcome;
4. general coding-performance improvement;
5. two correction rounds being generally sufficient;
6. the final attempt 24 product being acceptable because its tests passed;
7. the current role graph being minimal or economically justified; or
8. perfect causal isolation merely because structural context boundaries
   exist.

## Why failed attempts remain useful

The cohort distinguished model, provider, runner, product, and telemetry
failures instead of collapsing all stops into “the agent failed.” It also
showed that a run may be nonaccepting while still providing valid mechanism
evidence.

That is the point of failure preservation: retain enough structure to learn
what happened without laundering failure into success or letting a failed
product become successor authority.
