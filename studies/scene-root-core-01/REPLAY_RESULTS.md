## Evidence-class boundary

Fork replay reconstructs a retained natural decision point, changes one
registered condition, and runs a bounded segment forward. Controls run first,
synthetic arms stay visibly synthetic, and replay rows never pool with the
natural trajectory that supplied the fork.

## Instrument honesty before use

Reconstruction was verified per seat type. Unverified seats were refused
rather than approximated. One preregistered arm proved incompatible with the
real interface and shipped as a typed guard-refusal, with the evidence that
blocked it retained.

These refusals are instrument results: inability to run an arm honestly does
not authorize redesign after seeing the problem.

## First preregistered set

The fork, binding, arms, observables, reporting order, and fences were fixed
before execution. The unmodified controls were reconstructed byte-faithfully
and completed before the announcement arm.

The controls showed that the retained natural behavior was common under that
binding rather than interpretable from a single trajectory. The announcement
shifted where actors routed the same surfaced need. That is a descriptive
behavior contrast, not evidence that served research changed later work.

Inherited false evidence did not induce dishonest assertion in the observed
set. A single evaluator scored that behavior, so the finding remains bounded.

One downstream observable named in the registration could not be collected by
the instrument. It remains `UNMEASURED`, not zero. A possible replay-leakage
path was retained as an instrument repair even though no trace showed use.

Exact arm sizes, rates, timings, model bindings, and statistical values remain
in private custody.

## Preregistered fences

- The set cannot answer an affordance-removal hypothesis because the arm that
  would remove the affordance was inadmissible.
- It cannot answer whether served evidence changes writing because delivery
  occurred downstream of the measured output.
- No replay observation becomes a naturalistic or provider claim.

## Judge-panel rounds

The shadow record carried a single-evaluator caveat. Arm-blind panel rounds
tested that caveat on identical retained packets.

The first round found substantial agreement among capable judges and one
objective miss by the weakest binding. It also exposed a packet-context
confound in comparison with the original evaluator.

The second round supplied self-contained evidence and separated that confound:
capable bindings substantially reproduced the retained record, while the weak
binding miscalibrated in both directions. It is therefore blocked from solo
adjudication on this instrument. The retained record also gained a
context-sensitive recalibration flag rather than a retrospective rewrite.

For future gating: use evidence-complete packets and a capable panel. Review
rounds calibrate this instrument only; they do not rank models generally.

## Limits

The replay set covers one fork and one verified seat type. Panel packets cover
one adjudication rubric. Missing observables stay missing, all scored rows
retain their evaluator caveats, and nothing here establishes improved work.
