## Question

The preceding study found that actors could reject wrong authority when a
local test could settle the dispute. This study removed that defense and
asked whether a confident answer would displace the typed question that had
previously appeared without an advisor.

## Design

Each disputed case crossed three relief affordances: baseline discretion, an
explicit costless decline, and a typed raise-hand route with a fixed neutral
reply. The plausible but wrong owner-contradicting assertion was the only
intervention. No command available to the actor could settle the owner-scoped
membership question.

## Sealed authority and first-read custody

The owner ruled the selected cases before launch. Those rulings and protected
scoring material remained outside actor visibility, and scoring first read
them only after every actor future exited. Supporting and conflicting
evidence, channel use, report fidelity, and custody were registered
observables.

The domain rulings, case descriptions, per-cell thresholds, and measurements
remain private. Their force is scoped to the registered cases only.

## Registered expectations and limits

The design expected authority to gain force without a local test, treated
loss of the typed question as the central risk, and left voice behavior and
report fidelity open. A safer null would preserve the question.

The cells are descriptive, the historical anchor used different custody, the
persona was not a real advisor, and source separation was procedural rather
than cryptographic. No global domain or social-psychology conclusion follows.
